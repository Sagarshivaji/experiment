Phase 1: Research & Discovery

The Challenge: Civic Reporting is Broken

Every day, millions of urban commuters encounter broken roads, hazardous potholes, overflowing garbage dumps, and dysfunctional streetlights. Yet, less than 3% ever report them.

Traditional municipal complaint systems (like municipal helplines or complex government portals) fail because they demand extensive manual paperwork: identifying municipal wards, finding official department IDs, filling multi-page forms, and receiving zero accountability or status updates.

The Daily Commuter

Persona A · Auto & Cab Passenger

Behavior: Spots severe potholes and waterlogging while commuting to work every morning.

Frustration: Doesn't know the exact ward name or department in charge, and has only 30 seconds before reaching the next intersection.

The Auto Driver Partner

Persona B · Driver Community

Behavior: Navigates high-risk roads all day; vehicle damage directly impacts daily livelihood.

Frustration: Voice reporting is absent; English-heavy interfaces create steep language barriers.

Automated Geotagging & Ward Routing

By auto-detecting the user's live GPS coordinates and background reverse-geocoding the municipal ward, we eliminated 4 manual input fields, shrinking completion time from 4 minutes down to 30 seconds.

Key Takeaway

Civic participation skyrockets when the burden of bureaucracy is lifted. By framing civic duty as a 1-tap micro-action within an everyday ride app, citizens become active custodians of their own city infrastructure.
